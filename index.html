A1 · Summary

A launch-ready MVP on 29 January, the full 17-item scope by the end of February.

That leaves almost four months of runway before Father's Day, Vinyly's biggest gifting moment. The plan rests on three decisions.

Buy the commodity, build the magic

Shopify runs the catalogue, checkout, payments, promo codes, accounts and refunds. The build effort goes into what makes Vinyly different: the configurator, live preview, music pipeline and Vinyly AI.

Production-ready from day one

Every paid order produces print-ready artwork and mastered audio, handed to the pressing partner from an ops console. A beautiful site that can't feed the factory is not a product.

De-risk the hard parts early

Spotify's API rules, music licensing and runaway AI costs could each sink the launch if left late. Each has a plan below, starting in week one.

A4 · Architecture

A thin custom core between proven services.

A TypeScript stack on managed services. The Studio API owns everything about the record; Shopify owns everything about the money; a paid-order webhook joins the two.

One design file, two renderers. The browser draws the live preview; a server worker re-renders the same design at print resolution when the order is paid. The preview cannot drift from what gets pressed.

Built to run

  • Uploads go straight to storage in resumable chunks, so a 200 MB WAV survives a flaky mobile connection.
  • AI styling is queued, cached and capped at three levels, with a monthly ceiling that pauses AI rather than overspend.
  • Error tracking, uptime alerts and cost dashboards from launch day.

A6 · Risks

The two biggest risks are not code: Spotify access and music licensing.

MWT Labs would rather name them now than discover them in month four.

RiskImpactHow MWT Labs handles it
Spotify API access"Create a vinyl with any playlist" may not work for Spotify users at launch: since February 2026, apps in Spotify's Development Mode are limited to five users, and extended access is aimed at established services.Launch with Apple Music import, track search and uploads. Build Spotify behind the same tracklist model and switch it on when approved. Apply for access in week one.
Music licensingPressing commercial recordings needs rights from the publisher and the recording's owner. Streaming services provide no audio for pressing.Audio ID flags every commercial track; ops can hold, approve or refuse. Buyers confirm rights to their uploads. Vinyly takes legal advice on its licensing model before launch.
AI cost overrunMargin erosion from heavy usersCaching, three-level caps, a monthly ceiling and a swappable provider adapter.
Pressing spec unknownRework of print and audio outputGet the partner's spec in Stage 1; test-press records before launch.
Occasion peaksTraffic spikes around Valentine's, Mother's Day, Father's Day and ChristmasShopify checkout and a serverless front end scale on demand; load test before the first peak.

Assumptions

  • Vinyly supplies brand assets, copy and product photography.
  • Vinyly provides one company email; MWT Labs sets up and manages all accounts, environments and DevOps from there.
  • One pressing partner at launch, with file delivery rather than a live API.
  • English and GBP at launch; UK and EU shipping handled by Shopify.
  • Third-party fees (Shopify, hosting, AI, audio ID) are paid by Vinyly.

Questions for Stage 1

  1. Who is your pressing partner, and what are their artwork and audio specs, minimum runs and lead times?
  2. What is your licensing model for commercial tracks: licence per order, own recordings only, or a hybrid?
  3. Should Heart Shape and Full Print share one price, or vary by format and side length?
  4. Is Vinyly Song Creator needed at launch or later?
